Common MSP SEO Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)

By Alex | Published September 9, 2026 | 7 min read

We look at a lot of MSP websites, and the same handful of SEO mistakes show up over and over.

None of them are complicated to fix. Most of them just take being a little more specific, a little more consistent, and a little more honest about what you’re actually measuring.

That’s exactly what our SEO for MSPs service is built to sort out.

Here are the five we see most often, and exactly what to do instead.

1Trying to Be Everything to Everyone

× Wrong

“We provide IT services for all businesses.”

✓ Right

“We specialize in HIPAA-compliant IT for healthcare practices in [City].”

This is the one we see the most, and it’s the one that costs MSPs the most money.

When you say “we provide IT services for all businesses,” you’re not actually saying anything. A dental practice looking for HIPAA-compliant IT support has no reason to believe you understand their world any better than the next generic provider on the results page. You sound exactly like everyone else, because you’re saying exactly what everyone else says.

Specialists charge more and convert better than generalists. That’s not a branding opinion, that’s just how buyers behave.

Someone searching for “HIPAA compliant IT support for healthcare” already knows what they need. If your site speaks directly to that—using their language, naming their compliance requirements, calling out their specific pain points—you immediately look like the safer, more credible choice, before you’ve said a single word to them on a call.

You don’t have to abandon your other clients to do this. Pick one or two verticals where you already have real experience (healthcare, legal, finance, whatever it is) and build dedicated pages, dedicated messaging, and dedicated proof points around them. You can still serve everyone. You just don’t have to market to everyone the same way.

2Ignoring Mobile Optimization

67%

of B2B searches now happen on mobile. If your site isn’t mobile-friendly, you’re invisible to most of your prospects, even if you’re ranking well on desktop.

Here’s a quick test you can run right now: pull out your phone and search your main keyword, something like “managed IT services [your city].” Now compare how your site loads and reads against your competitors.

  • Is the text actually readable without zooming in?
  • Does the page load in a couple of seconds, or does it hang?
  • Can someone tap your phone number or fill out your form without fighting the layout?

A lot of MSP sites were built years ago on desktop-first templates and never got a real second look for mobile. It shows immediately the moment you pull them up on a phone.

This isn’t just a ranking factor either. Even if Google sends someone to your site, if the experience is clunky on their phone, they’re gone in seconds. You paid for that visibility—through SEO effort or ad spend—and then lost the visitor to a bad mobile experience. Fix the foundation before you worry about anything else.

3Focusing on Vanity Metrics

× Vanity Metrics

Page views, time on site, bounce rate

✓ Revenue Metrics

Form submissions, phone calls, qualified leads

We get it, it feels good to open Google Analytics and see traffic climbing month over month. But traffic on its own doesn’t pay your bills. We’ve seen MSP sites with steadily rising page views and a flat, sometimes declining, number of actual leads.

Page views, time on site, and bounce rate tell you how people are browsing. They don’t tell you whether any of it is turning into revenue. You can have a beautifully “engaging” site that nobody ever calls.

Track what actually matters: inquiries from qualified prospects in your service area. That means form submissions, phone calls, and ideally how many of those inquiries turn into real conversations and proposals.

If you’re only reporting on traffic, you’re optimizing for the wrong outcome—and you’ll keep making decisions based on numbers that don’t connect to whether your pipeline is actually growing.

4Inconsistent Content Publishing

Publishing five blog posts in one month, then nothing for three months, is worse than publishing one post consistently every month.

This happens constantly. Someone gets motivated, cranks out a handful of posts in a burst, and then life (or client work) takes over and the blog goes quiet for a quarter. From an SEO standpoint, that stop-start pattern does very little for you. Search engines reward consistency and depth over time, not short bursts of activity followed by silence.

The fix: build a simple content calendar and stick to it, even if that means publishing less often than you’d like. One solid, useful post a month, every month, will outperform a burst-and-abandon pattern almost every time. Consistency beats intensity here. If once a month is realistic for your team, commit to that and actually hit it, rather than promising yourself weekly posts you know you won’t keep up with.

5Neglecting Technical SEO

Your site needs to load fast (under 3 seconds), be mobile-friendly, and have a proper URL structure. These aren’t optional extras or advanced techniques—they’re table stakes.

We still see MSP sites with page load times well past that 3-second mark, messy URL structures that don’t reflect the actual page content, and basic technical issues quietly working against every other piece of marketing being done. You can write great content and build strong location pages, but if the technical foundation underneath it is broken, you’re fighting an uphill battle you don’t need to be fighting.

Three quick checks to run today:

  • Run your site through a basic speed test
  • Check your URLs actually make sense—a clean structure like /services/managed-it-services/ beats a jumbled string of parameters
  • Confirm mobile responsiveness is actually solid, not just technically passing a test

These fixes aren’t glamorous, but they’re foundational. Nothing else you do in SEO works as well if this part is broken.

Putting It All Together

None of these mistakes are hard to fix on their own. What trips most MSPs up is treating SEO as a side project instead of an actual channel with its own standards:

  • Be specific about who you serve
  • Make sure the site actually works on the device most people are using
  • Measure what actually feeds your pipeline
  • Publish consistently instead of in bursts
  • Get the technical basics right before layering anything else on top
