Why Most MSPs Fail to Rank in the Google 3-Pack (And How to Fix It)

By Alex | Published November 9, 2026 | 9 min read

Pull out your phone and search “IT support near me.”

Three businesses show up in the map pack. Odds are, yours isn’t one of them.

And that’s a problem, because those three listings get most of the calls from local businesses that are actively looking for a provider. That includes the ones who want co-managed IT, a one-off project, or just a better MSP than the one they have.

Here’s what I see over and over. An MSP fills in the business name and the website URL, picks “Computer Support and Services,” hits verify, and never touches the profile again.

Then the owner wonders why the phone only rings when a referral comes in.

The good news is that this is one of the most fixable problems in MSP marketing. You don’t need a big budget. You need to stop making four specific mistakes.

The 4 Mistakes, In Order
  1. Running on one category
  2. Setting up your address wrong
  3. Sending profile traffic to your homepage
  4. Collecting reviews with no system

1Running on One Category

Your categories tell Google what kind of business you are, and so what searches you should show up for.

Your profile gets one primary category and up to nine additional ones. Most MSPs use the primary slot and leave the rest empty.

1 + 9

is how many categories Google lets you set: one primary and up to nine additional. Most MSPs use one.

The primary category is the main one. It’s the label Google leans on most when it decides what you are, so it should match the service you most want to be found for.

If you sell managed IT, that’s Managed Service Provider, not the generic option most people pick by default.

Then use the additional slots for the other things you really do. For a typical MSP, that might look like this:

✕What most MSPs do

Primary: Computer Support and Services. Additional: nothing.

✓What works better

Primary: Managed Service Provider. Additional: Computer Support and Services, Computer Security Service, Data Recovery Service.

Why does this help? Each category maps to a different kind of search.

  • Managed Service Provider covers “managed IT services” and “MSP near me.”
  • Computer Support and Services covers the broader “IT support near me” searches, including co-managed and project work.
  • Computer Security Service covers “cybersecurity company” and compliance-driven searches.
  • Data Recovery Service covers backup and recovery searches, which are often the panic searches that turn into clients.

One warning, though. Don’t go category-crazy. Google’s own guidance says to use as few categories as it takes to describe your core business, and not to add one for every service.

Only add a category if it’s something you genuinely do. If you don’t offer data recovery as a real service, skip it.

The fix: make your primary category match your core offer, then add two to four additional categories for services you actually deliver.

Category names change from time to time, so check the live list inside your profile when you edit.

2Setting Up Your Address Wrong

This is the one that gets profiles suspended, and it’s the one MSPs get wrong most often.

Many MSPs don’t have a storefront where clients walk in. So they grab a virtual office address, or a coworking space, or they use the owner’s home address, and then set it up the wrong way.

Here’s what Google’s guidelines say. A virtual office or a mailbox isn’t eligible just because you can receive mail there.

A listed location has to be staffed by your team during your stated business hours. If nobody from your company is actually there, you’re at risk.

The same rule applies to coworking spaces. You need clear signage, and you need your own staff there during opening hours.

So what do you do if you work out of a home office or serve clients at their sites?

You set yourself up as a service-area business. That means you hide your address and show the area you serve instead.

If you’re a service-area business, Google says to hide your address. Once it’s hidden, your profile only shows the service area.

Here are the traps I’d watch for:

  • An unstaffed virtual office listed as a storefront. This is the classic one, and it gets suspended.
  • A home address showing publicly. It’s not just a privacy problem. If you aren’t seeing customers there, it shouldn’t be displayed.
  • A service area that’s too big. Google says it shouldn’t extend much farther than about two hours of driving from where you’re based. Listing the whole state doesn’t make you show up statewide.
  • Different details everywhere. If your address and phone number differ between your profile, your website, and the directories, you’re sending Google mixed signals.

The fix: decide which kind of business you really are. If clients visit a staffed office, list it. If not, hide the address, set your service area, and keep your name, address, and phone number identical everywhere.

If your profile is already suspended, fix the underlying problem first, then go through Google’s guidelines before you reinstate it. Resubmitting without fixing it just gets you suspended again.

3Sending Profile Traffic to Your Homepage

Look at the website link on your profile. Where does it go?

For most MSPs, it’s the homepage. That’s a missed opportunity.

Think about who clicks that link. Someone searched “managed IT services” in your city, saw your listing, and tapped through.

If they land on a homepage that talks about everything you do, for everyone, in every city, they have to hunt for the thing they came for. A lot of them just leave.

Instead, send them to a page that matches the search. A local landing page for your core service, written for that city, with local proof and a clear way to get in touch.

✕Generic

Your homepage, with ten services and no mention of the searcher’s city.

✓Matched

“Managed IT Services in Denver”: one service, one city, one clear next step.

It’s the same idea I talk about with paid ads. Match the page to the intent, and more people take the next step.

If you’re running Google Ads too, I covered the same principle in how to run Google Ads for your MSP. And if you want a closer look at what a page like this should include, our MSP website design page covers it.

Track it properly in GA4

Once you’ve got the link pointing to the right page, you want to know what it’s producing. By default, traffic from your profile just blends in with everything else.

The fix is to add UTM parameters to the website link in your profile. Here’s a simple version:

Example link: yourdomain.com/managed-it-services-denver/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp

With that in place, GA4 can show you how much traffic comes from your profile, which pages those visitors land on, and whether they fill out a form or call.

Open your traffic acquisition report and add the session campaign as a secondary dimension. Then look for “gbp.”

Pair that with the performance numbers inside the profile itself, like calls and direction requests, and you can finally see what this channel is worth.

Two quick notes. Keep the UTM names consistent, because “GBP,” “gbp,” and “google-profile” will show up as three different campaigns. And only use one UTM link per page, so your data doesn’t get split.

4Collecting Reviews With No System

Reviews are one of the biggest reasons the top three look the way they do. And most MSPs treat them as an afterthought.

They ask for one when a client happens to say something nice, and then go quiet for six months. Then they wonder why the competitor down the road has 90 reviews.

What matters isn’t just the total. It’s review velocity, meaning how steadily new reviews come in, and how recent they are.

A steady trickle looks natural. Forty reviews in a week followed by nothing for a year does not.

So build a system. The easiest trigger for an MSP is the ticket or project closing.

  1. Pick the trigger. A resolved ticket, a finished project, or a completed onboarding.
  2. Automate the ask. Most PSAs and ticketing tools can send a short follow-up email or text when a ticket closes, with your direct review link.
  3. Keep the message short. Thank them, say it takes a minute, and include the link.
  4. Reply to every review. Positive or negative, within a day or two.

Don’t break Google’s rules while you do it

This is where a lot of MSPs get nervous, so let me be clear about it.

You’re allowed to ask for reviews. What you can’t do is game the process.

Google’s policy says businesses shouldn’t discourage negative reviews or selectively solicit positive ones. It also prohibits offering anything in exchange for a review, like a discount or a gift card.

That means no review gating. Don’t send a “how did we do?” survey and only forward the happy people to Google. Everybody who closes a ticket gets the same ask.

It also means no incentives, no telling people what to write, and no giving your staff a quota of reviews to collect.

If someone has a bad experience, deal with it separately. Reply publicly, take it offline, and fix the problem.

So how do you get keyword-rich reviews?

You can’t ask customers to include certain words. Google’s policy says not to request specific content in a review.

But you can still get there the honest way.

First, deliver the services you want to be known for. Customers who get help with cybersecurity or backup will naturally mention it.

Second, use your replies. When you thank someone, mention what you helped with and where, like “Glad we could get your Denver office set up with a better backup plan.” That’s natural, it’s helpful to the next reader, and it puts your service and city on the page.

The fix: trigger the review ask from closed tickets, send it to everyone, reply to every review, and let your service and city show up naturally in your responses.

Putting It All Together

If your profile isn’t showing in the 3-pack, you probably don’t need more tricks. You need to fix the basics you skipped.

  • Set a primary category that matches your core offer, and add a few real additional categories.
  • Use a staffed address, or hide it and set a service area. Keep your details consistent everywhere.
  • Point your profile link at a local landing page, with UTM tracking so you can see the results in GA4.
  • Ask every client for a review at a set moment, with no gating and no incentives, and reply to all of them.

Do these four things and you’ll be ahead of most MSPs in your market.
