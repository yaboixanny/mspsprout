6 Ways to Improve Your MSP Marketing

By Alex | Published October 25, 2026 | 11 min read

Most MSPs I talk to aren’t actually short on marketing ideas. They’ve tried SEO, they’ve dabbled in LinkedIn, maybe they ran a few Google Ads campaigns last year.

The problem isn’t a lack of tactics. It’s that most of those tactics were run in isolation, for a few weeks, without ever being tied together into something that actually compounds.

This checklist is the six things I’d actually fix first if you handed me your MSP marketing today. Not forty tactics. Six, done properly, in the right order.

The 6 Fixes, In Order
  1. Lock in your positioning and target market
  2. Fix your website so it converts
  3. Build real SEO and content
  4. Decide deliberately on paid search
  5. Build a lead generation engine with more than one moving part
  6. Track what actually matters

1Lock In Your Positioning and Target Market First

I’ll say this bluntly: most of what looks like a “marketing problem” at an MSP is actually a positioning problem wearing a marketing costume.

If your messaging still reads as “comprehensive IT solutions for businesses of all sizes,” none of the other five items on this list are going to work as well as they should. You’re spending money and effort to be generically visible instead of specifically chosen.

×Generic

“Comprehensive IT solutions for businesses of all sizes.”

✓Specific

“Managed IT and HIPAA compliance for medical and dental practices.”

Target market and segment selection sounds like a strategy slide, but it’s really just answering one question honestly: who do you actually want calling you, and what do they need to hear to believe you’re the right fit?

Healthcare practices worried about HIPAA compliance need completely different proof than a 40-person law firm worried about ransomware. A website trying to speak to both at once usually speaks clearly to neither.

The fix: pick two or three verticals where you already have real client experience. Build your service pages, your case studies, and your ad copy around those specific buyers instead of “any business with a computer.”

This single decision makes every other item on this list cheaper and more effective, because you’re no longer paying to reach everyone in order to convince a fraction of them.

2Fix Your Website So It Actually Converts

A lot of MSP websites were built once, three or four years ago, and never touched again except for the occasional team photo update. Meanwhile, the way buyers actually use a website has changed considerably.

MSP website essentials come down to a handful of things that matter more than they get credit for:

  • A homepage that states who you serve and what changes for them
  • Service pages organized by what the buyer actually searches for, not how your internal team is structured
  • Real case studies with specific numbers
  • A clear path from “I’m reading this” to “I just booked a call”

If someone has to hunt for your phone number or guess what city you actually service, you’re losing leads your other marketing already paid to generate.

This is also where technical SEO quietly either helps or sabotages everything else you do. A slow-loading site, broken internal links, or pages that confuse search engines about what they’re actually about will undercut every dollar you spend on content or ads pointed at that same site.

Before you invest further anywhere else on this list, confirm your site is fast, mobile-friendly, and structured in a way that’s actually crawlable. Should an MSP publish pricing on its website? My honest take is that some form of pricing transparency, even just a “starting from” range or a breakdown of what factors into cost, converts better than hiding it entirely.

Buyers comparing MSPs hate walking into a sales call completely blind on budget. You don’t need a full price list, but total silence on the topic usually costs you more qualified leads than it protects.

3Build Real SEO and Content, Not Just a Blog Nobody Reads

Content marketing and SEO show up on almost every MSP marketing list you’ll ever read, and for good reason. But the execution is where most MSPs go wrong.

They publish generically, inconsistently, or purely for keyword volume instead of for the specific questions their buyers are actually asking.

Search intent matters more here than most MSPs realize. Someone searching “managed IT services pricing” is at a completely different stage than someone searching “what does managed IT include,” and your content needs to match that intent instead of funneling every visitor toward the same generic “contact us” page.

Here’s where I’d actually focus:

  • Write in real depth about the specific problems your best clients hired you to solve, not generic “5 Cybersecurity Tips” posts that could’ve been written by any MSP in the country
  • Build genuine case studies with real, specific numbers wherever a client will let you share them
  • Structure service pages so they answer the exact question a buyer typed into Google, including the practical details most competitors skip (what’s included, what’s not, how onboarding actually works)

4Decide Deliberately on Paid Search, Not by Accident

Is paid search actually worth it for MSPs? Yes, but only once the groundwork above is in place.

I’ve watched plenty of MSPs spend real money on Google Ads only to send that traffic to a generic homepage with no clear next step, and then conclude the channel “doesn’t work.” The channel worked fine. The landing page it pointed to didn’t.

Paid search and PPC advertising are genuinely one of the fastest ways for an MSP to generate new leads, because you’re reaching people actively searching for exactly what you offer, right now, rather than waiting for organic content to build authority over months.

The tradeoff is cost. MSP keywords are competitive and clicks aren’t cheap, so budget and patience both matter here.

If you want to run it yourself, start with our step-by-step guide to running Google Ads for your MSP. If you’d rather have it managed, that’s our Google Ads for MSPs service.

Should MSPs bother with traditional advertising like billboards or radio? For most MSPs, no.

Those channels are built for broad brand awareness, and an MSP’s buyer pool in any given metro is narrow enough that you’re paying to reach a huge number of people who will never need your specific service.

Your budget is almost always better spent on channels where you can target by intent (search) or by specific company attributes (LinkedIn), rather than by sheer geographic reach.

5Build a Lead Generation Engine With More Than One Moving Part

Lead generation strategies for MSPs work best stacked, not swapped. A lot of owners chase whichever channel is trendiest this quarter, drop whatever they were doing before, and wonder why nothing ever compounds.

A real engine usually includes local SEO, a consistent referral and networking presence, LinkedIn content and outreach aimed at the right personas, and some form of paid search or social advertising layered on top once the fundamentals are solid.

None of these need to be huge individually.

5:1

Five leads a month from five different channels, closing one, is a completely normal and sustainable way to grow an MSP from zero.

Which channels actually produce the fastest results? Paid search and direct outbound tend to produce the quickest wins, because they’re reaching people with existing intent or a specific trigger event (a contract renewal, a recent security scare) rather than waiting for them to stumble onto your content.

SEO and referrals take longer to build but tend to produce cheaper, more durable leads once they’re established. The right answer is almost always both, running at the same time, not one instead of the other.

How can an MSP get found in local search results?

Start with a fully filled-out Google Business Profile, listing every service category accurately rather than a generic “computer repair” label.

Layer in consistent business information across every directory you’re listed on, actively collect and respond to reviews, and build location-specific content if you serve more than one city or region.

Review directories worth prioritizing are the ones your actual buyers check before calling: Google, your industry-specific directories, and any local chamber of commerce listings that carry real weight in your market.

6Track What Actually Matters, Not Just What’s Easy to Measure

Tracking and measuring marketing performance is where a lot of MSPs either skip the work entirely or measure the wrong things.

Page views and impressions feel good to report on, but they don’t pay your bills. Qualified leads, booked calls, and closed clients do.

Your CRM and PSA tools matter more here than people give them credit for.

Which CRM should an MSP use? Honestly, the specific platform matters less than whether you actually use it consistently to track where every lead came from, what stage they’re at, and whether marketing spend is turning into real pipeline. A properly used free CRM beats an expensive one nobody logs into.

How do you evaluate and adjust your strategy over time? Review it on a real cadence, monthly at minimum, and ask the same honest questions every time:

  • Which channels actually produced qualified conversations this month?
  • Which ones just produced traffic or impressions?
  • Where is budget going that isn’t earning its place?

Cut or adjust what isn’t working instead of letting it quietly keep draining budget out of habit.

Should MSPs invest in brand awareness marketing on top of all this? In small doses, yes, mostly through consistent content and social presence rather than big broad campaigns.

Brand awareness compounds slowly in the background while your more direct channels (search, outbound, referrals) do the immediate heavy lifting. It’s a long-term multiplier, not a lead generation tactic on its own, so it should never be the first thing you invest in if your pipeline is currently thin.

Putting It All Together

None of these six things work especially well in isolation:

  • Positioning without a converting website wastes the clarity you built
  • A great website without real content and SEO behind it has nothing bringing people to it
  • Paid search without a stacked lead generation engine behind it is just one channel carrying all the weight
  • And none of it means much if you’re not tracking what’s working well enough to keep doing it

Fix these six in order, and most of the “marketing doesn’t work for MSPs” feeling disappears, because the problem usually was never the channel. It was the sequence.
