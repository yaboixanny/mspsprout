Why Selling Software to MSPs Is Different

MSPs are skeptical, busy, and loyal to the tools they already run. A generic SaaS playbook misses how they actually buy.

People

More Than One Decision Maker

The owner approves the spend. The lead technician decides whether the tool actually works in the stack. Your marketing has to convince both, and they care about different things: margin and risk for one, reliability and ease of use for the other.

Peers

Peers Outweigh Ads

MSPs ask each other what to buy in peer groups, forums, and communities. A recommendation from another MSP beats an ad, so your marketing has to earn those mentions as well as the click.

Channel

Partner Programs Matter

Many MSPs buy through vendor partner programs, distributors, and marketplaces. Partner and channel marketing needs its own plan alongside your direct funnel.

Trust

Trust Beats Features

Software at the center of an MSP’s stack is painful to switch. They want proof it’s reliable, well supported, and here to stay, so case studies, integrations, and honest pricing matter more than a long feature list.

The Software We Market to MSPs

Each category has a different buyer worry, so each gets a different message.

RMM & PSA Platforms

These sit at the foundation of an MSP’s stack, so switching costs are high. Buyers want migration help, integrations, and proof from MSPs their size before they’ll even trial.

Security Tools

MSPs often resell these, so margin, partner pricing, and vendor consolidation matter as much as detection quality. Compliance proof helps close the deal.

Backup & Recovery

Trust is the product. MSPs want evidence the tool restores when it counts, plus support they can reach on a bad day.

Documentation & Workflow Tools

The pitch is time saved and onboarding made easy. Integrations with the PSA and RMM they already run usually decide it.

How to Choose a SaaS Marketing Agency

Here’s how to judge any agency, including us, before you sign.

Red Flags

  • No understanding of how MSPs or IT buyers actually make purchases
  • Guaranteed demos, rankings, or revenue
  • Reports built on traffic and impressions instead of pipeline
  • A funnel copied from consumer SaaS
  • Unclear ownership of ad accounts, content, and data
  • Long lock-in contracts with no way out

Questions to Ask

  • How do you think MSPs research and choose software?
  • How will you measure demos and pipeline, not just leads?
  • What happens in the first 90 days?
  • Who owns the ad accounts, content, and data if we part ways?
  • How long is the contract, and how do we exit?
  • Who does the day-to-day work on each channel?

SaaS Marketing FAQs

The questions software vendors ask most before starting with us.

How long until I see results?
It depends on the channel. Paid search and LinkedIn can start producing demo requests within weeks, while SEO usually shows first ranking movement within about 90 days and compounds after that. B2B software sold to MSPs also has a real sales cycle, so we track pipeline and demos along the way, not just closed revenue.
Should we hire an agency or build marketing in-house?
In-house gives you control and deep product knowledge, but it takes time to hire and ramp a team across SEO, paid, content, and ops. An agency gets channels running faster and brings experience with MSP buyers. Many software vendors start with an agency or a fractional CMO, then bring pieces in-house as they grow.
Do you require a long-term contract?
Contract length depends on the scope of work. Whatever you decide, get the term, the exit terms, and what you own if you leave in writing before you start. We’ll walk through all of it on the first call.
Should we start with SEO or paid?
Usually both, in a different order depending on your budget and runway. Paid search and LinkedIn give you fast signal on which messages and offers work. SEO and content build lower-cost demand over months. Early stage teams often lead with paid to learn faster, then layer in SEO.
How do you use AI in content?
Google’s guidance rewards content that is helpful and trustworthy, whatever tools helped produce it. Every piece is built around real questions MSP buyers ask, and you review and approve it before it publishes.
Is there a free audit?
Book a call and we’ll walk through your site, your demo flow, and your channels, then tell you where we’d start. If you want a formal written audit, ask us about scope on the call.
Who will I work with?
Your first call is with Alex, our founder, who has run SEO and PPC campaigns since 2012. It’s a fair question for any agency, so ask who will do the day-to-day work on each channel before you sign.
What is a B2B SaaS go-to-market strategy?
It’s your plan for reaching and winning customers: who your ideal customer is, how you position against alternatives, how you price and package, which channels you use, and how your sales motion and metrics fit together. For software sold to MSPs, it also covers partner programs and peer communities, which carry more weight than they do in most markets.
